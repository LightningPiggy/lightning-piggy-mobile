= Troubleshooting

[cols="1,2", options="header"]
|===
| Problem | Solution

| Found logs, map Piglets or app-relay reads stay empty after account hydration, or keep using a switched-off relay
| A context relay array can change while its URL key stays identical (for example, when personal relays are configured and a geo-cache or marketplace switch changes). Effects that capture app relay groups must also depend on `useAppRelayPrefs()`, which observes active-account changes, hydration and persisted edits. Close old subscriptions and cancel pending reads before re-arming; keep current cache logs and unsaved settings drafts across preference edits. Nearby viewport subscriptions include the preference scope in their identity. Marmot refreshes group connections without rebuilding MLS state, and invalidates cached fallback inbox targets. Regressions: `appRelaySubscriptionLifecycle.test.ts`, `appRelayReadLifecycle.test.ts`, settings/feed tests and `MarmotBridge.relayPrefs.test.ts`.

| App relays stay empty after a temporary preference-storage failure, or background account work uses another account's switches
| Preference hydration errors keep app defaults closed. Ordinary relay reads retry failed loads after a one-second cooldown, sharing an in-flight load and notifying consumers only after success. The active-account hook also retries every three seconds until success, cancelling queued retries on switch or unmount. New-account contact-list setup awaits hydration before selecting defaults. Account-owned Marmot, NIP-17 and marketplace operations pass their owning identity to preference lookups across account changes. Regression coverage: `appRelayPrefs.test.ts`, `appRelayConsumers.test.ts`, `marmotNetwork.test.ts` and the invitation-key discovery/retirement tests.

| Marmot says "You added 2 devices (Unknown app ×2)" while the other participant sees their app names
| The inviter caches kind-30443 `client` tags against MLS leaf signature keys. A receiving install sees only the Add commit's leaves, and only its own current leaf has a built-in app name; its account's other installs need the same key-package lookup as anyone else's. The notices hook now starts the existing bounded, cooldown-protected read-only lookup on focus and a newly observed epoch, without requiring the device-details sheet. Learned names trigger a reread, not another lookup. Notices persist signature keys, not labels, so existing notices update when a matching published package is found. An absent/replaced or untagged package stays unknown; the account pubkey never implies an app. This notice path uses key-package client tags, not MIP-06 device metadata. Regressions: `useMarmotDevices.test.ts`, `marmotDeviceTracker.test.ts` and the real MLS `marmotSessionDevices.test.ts`.

| A successful seller save disappears on a fresh device, or revision checks miss marketplace-only edits and deletions
| A marketplace relay can accept a publication even when every configured write relay rejects it. Seller snapshots and revision/deletion checks query marketplace publication targets plus all configured write relays in batches of at most eight, with test-account lockdown applied by author. In every batch, only the healthy seller write-relay subset determines quorum and successful responses; marketplace replies supplement the results. Marketplace timeouts cannot reject a successful seller quorum, and marketplace replies cannot replace a missing seller quorum. Regression: `src/services/marketSellerService.test.ts` covers each marketplace relay as the sole accepting relay, newer revisions, address/id deletions and locked reads.

| Signed-out shared events fail to load, or Android DMs keep using a disabled app relay
| Public network plans must retain app defaults when there is no active account. For a signed-in account, persist default-relay preference changes before syncing the Android background DM watch from its persisted enabled preference; React preference notifications alone do not replace its captured subscription. A re-arm alone cannot restart a host stopped after all relays were disabled, so re-enabling a relay must also start the preference-enabled host. Regression coverage: `useAppNetworkRelays.test.ts`, `appRelayPrefs.test.ts` and `appRelayPrefsBackgroundWatch.test.ts`. Seller snapshot and revision reads also filter app-provided fallback and marketplace relays while preserving explicitly chosen write relays; regressions: `marketSellerService.test.ts` and the source-consumer audit.

| A document attachment shows only “📎 File” or “📎 Attachment” on the first DM thread load, then becomes a card after reopening
| With an empty DM store, `fetchConversationFor` renders freshly ingested wrap entries before a stored-row read can supply the original content. Keep recognized kind-14 document URLs and kind-15 encoded attachment content in the entry's `renderText` while `text` retains the redacted inbox/notification label. Do not broaden this to wallet-share bearer secrets. Regression: `src/contexts/nostrFetchConversationAttachments.test.ts` exercises the real ingest and thread projection from an empty store, plus attachment classification and inbox redaction.

| Seller activity skips events across relays, or a busy listing blocks reviews and replies
| Relay query limits apply per relay. Select the globally newest limited page before deriving the inclusive timestamp boundary; an older event from a sparse relay must not move that boundary past unprocessed activity on a busy relay. Isolate failures per activity filter, finish the other coordinate/review/reply filters, and retain the checkpoint for retry when any filter remains incomplete. `sellerActivityService.test.ts` covers mixed relay histories and a saturated timestamp alongside independent activity.

| My shop → Orders or View order shows "No orders yet" despite a stored incoming Gamma order
| Seller screens must use `getShopOrderRows`, not buyer-scoped `getOrderRows` (which requires an outgoing placed order). Bound seller history by incoming placed orders and fetch all kind-16/17 lifecycle rows for each selected buyer/order pair. Verify the owner, buyer and direction against the encrypted DM rows; `useShopOrders.test.tsx`, `dmDbOrders.test.ts` and `MarketShopOrdersScreen.test.tsx` cover list loading, update bursts and detail routing.

| Android Marmot push alerts disappear after NIP-46 login, or background work replaces foreground relay routing
| DEV background routing must prefer a live foreground mirror, then the foreground legacy signer keys (NIP-46 uses the validated connection's `userPubkey`, not `identities_v1` or the legacy pubkey slot). Unknown/unreadable identities must not suppress generic push alerts; only known locked test accounts disable push alerts. Release builds skip routing hydration and push/location guards entirely; background sync retains its original registry lookup. Regressions cover all three release signers and missing/corrupt/locked DEV storage.

| DEV Nostr Connect QR pairing fails with "subscription closed before connection was established" after approval
| `nostr-tools` 2.23.3 closes the pairing subscription before setting its handshake success flag. The shared health-aware pool must retire the REQ immediately but defer the caller-initiated aggregate `onclose` callback to a Promise microtask, matching the SDK pool timing. A synchronous callback rejects even a valid encrypted acknowledgement. `healthAwarePool.test.ts` exercises the real `BunkerSigner.fromURI` with both health and resumable pool mixins, and checks that closing before an acknowledgement still rejects. DM subscription tests must likewise assert immediate REQ retirement, then await the Promise microtask before asserting the close callback; a synchronous callback assertion is stale after this fix.
| A Gamma order or payment update disappears after an interrupted encrypted-store write
| Admission counters reserve each rumor before ingest, but mark it stored only after the encrypted payload write succeeds. Live write failures and subscription teardown release delivery claims; batch write failures leave reservations retryable. Replayed reservations reuse their budget slot instead of blocking the rumor for 24 hours. Regression coverage: `orderAdmission.test.ts` and `sellerOrderGate.test.ts`.

| A seller cannot replace an expired Gamma invoice, or the buyer cannot pay a changed total
| Seller order detail offers Send invoice again once the locally recorded invoice expires. Confirmation checks settlement on the original receive wallet and blocks replacement when paid or unknown; an unpaid replacement retains the approved amount. Current shop prices must equal the placed order amount before any invoice is sent. Price/rate differences require a new agreed order until an amendment approval flow exists.

| "Unread messages" appears below the newest DM, or remains after everything is read
| React Native's inverted list cells use `column-reverse`, so a Fragment containing the divider and message reverses their visual order. Keep them inside a normal-column `View` (`ConversationScreen.tsx`). `utils/unreadDivider.ts` selects real incoming messages in reading order, excluding local device notices, failed invites and date headers. The shared `useChatReadPosition` hook keeps the opening boundary while unread messages remain and retires it when the live count reaches zero, for both DMs and groups. Do not recompute the boundary from the advancing live marker on every viewability report.

| Live DM logs `dropped by follow-gate` for someone in the newest Nostr follow list (#1458)
| The old contact loader skipped kind-3 reads for 24 hours on a fresh cache; its stale-cache refresh also kept the first relay response and ignored newer responses. Contact hydration now reconciles bounded kind-3 reads across user read relays and metadata indexers, selects by created_at (lowest event id on ties), and stores the follows and event version together. The dev log `[Nostr] follow set: N follows (kind-3 <created_at>)` identifies the adopted list. The live kind-4 and kind-1059 paths share an explicit hydration gate: cache paint does not mark follows ready. Incoming events (including old kind-4 backlog, silently) defer until reconciliation finishes; only fresh inbound arrivals carry notifications. After 15 seconds offline, buffered cached friends replay; unmatched entries remain in the capped recovery buffer so a slower reconciliation can still surface them. Reconciliation replays matches once and clears genuine strangers. Dev gate logs include partner prefix, follow count and hydration state for drops, or wire-envelope kind for deferrals. Comparisons use canonical hex keys. Round 2 logged disk adoption before `finish` scheduled the React contacts/readiness transition; live refs and replay depended on its later passive effect, so adoption could leave the gate stale. Adoption now synchronously publishes the account-owned set to both live paths, marks their shared hydration ref ready and drains the buffer directly (including already-known ids). Delayed UI renders cannot overwrite that set. The same adoption logs `[Nostr] follow-gate replay: N released, M dropped`; deferrals log `partner`, envelope `kind`, `follows`, `hydrated` and `inSet`. Round 3 lost pending entries because `nostrLiveDmSub` cleared the buffer on every subscription teardown, including startup relay-list re-opens. The module-level canonical follow-gate store now owns both refs and deferrals per viewer; only account switch/sign-out resets it. Adoption updates the canonical set, logs `follow set`, then replays. Every deferral includes `buffered=N`, and replay logs `start buffered=N` before released/dropped totals. A newly registered replay callback also drains entries if hydration finished while the sub was closed. Regressions: `src/contexts/useLiveFollowGate.test.ts` and `src/contexts/nostrLiveDmSubFollowGate.test.ts`. Regression: `src/services/contactFollowFreshness.test.ts` uses simulated relay responses, with no live relay access.

| Replies from someone you don't follow vanish from an open thread after you reply (#1623)
| Opening a thread fetches with the follow gate bypassed, so the first message shows and you can reply, but live arrivals went through `gateLiveDm`, which dropped a non-followed partner once follows had loaded. Their later messages and reactions never appeared (the dev log showed `dropped by follow-gate`). Sending a text or file message to someone now counts as accepting them: `src/services/dmRepliedPartners.ts` loads who you've messaged from the store (`selectRepliedConversations`, kinds 4 / 14 / 15, from you) and tracks new sends, and the live kind-4 / kind-1059 gates, the inbox refresh's store step and the background watch all pass those partners. The Messages list keeps its own trust tiers, so the thread still doesn't appear there for a non-followed partner. Regressions: `src/services/dmRepliedPartners.test.ts`, `src/contexts/liveDmRumorSurfaceReplied.test.ts`.

| Boltz reverse swap claim broadcast fails with "mandatory-script-verify-flag-failed (Witness program hash mismatch)"
| The Taproot internal key for Boltz v2 swaps is a MuSig2 aggregate of Boltz's key and the user's key — in a specific order. Per Boltz docs, **"Boltz's public key always coming first"**: for reverse swaps that means `keyAggregate([refundPubKey, claimPubKey])` (Boltz's refund key first, user's claim key second; **not sorted, not the other way round, not just refundPubKey alone**). Keys must be 33-byte compressed, aggregated via `@scure/btc-signer`'s `keyAggregate` + `keyAggExport`, then used as the x-only internal key when computing the Taproot tweak. See `boltzService.ts` `claimSwap()` and the Boltz docs at https://api.docs.boltz.exchange/claiming-swaps.html. Confirmed against the chain by computing `p2tr(internalKey, [claimLeaf, refundLeaf], undefined, true)` from `@scure/btc-signer` and matching its `.script` to the on-chain lockup scriptPubKey.

| Boltz reverse swap UI shows "Payment failed — Boltz swap failed: unknown Error" right after the Lightning balance drops
| First-attempt claim fails deterministically on a freshly created reverse swap. Symptoms: `[Boltz] Reverse swap created` followed almost immediately by `claimSwap` throwing `"unknown Error"` from `boltzService.ts`, while `payInvoiceForWallet` *did* succeed (Lightning balance dropped by the swap amount + fee). Root cause was tracked down in issue #481: the Alby SDK wraps NIP-47 responses with empty error bodies as `Nip47WalletError("unknown Error", "INTERNAL")` *even when the wallet processed the payment*. `nwcService.payInvoice` now looks up the invoice on that error code and returns the preimage if found. **Funds are not lost** even when the lookup fails: after the #481 fix both `SendSheet` and `TransferSheet` persist the full swap state to SecureStore (`boltz_swap_${id}`) and register it with `swapRecoveryService` *before* paying the invoice, so the next launch (or "Retry now" on TransferSheet) re-broadcasts the claim and settles the on-chain side. The `[SwapRecovery] Swap <id> claimed successfully` log line is the marker. If you ever see this overlay, force-stop and relaunch — the funds will land. See `docs/SOLUTION_DESIGN.adoc` → "Persistence + Recovery contract" for the full handshake.

| `SendSheet` reverse swap leaves stuck on-chain HTLC with no recovery path (before the #481 fix)
| Versions before issue #481 was fixed only persisted the swap secrets in JS memory in `SendSheet` (TransferSheet always persisted to SecureStore). When `claimSwap` failed, the random preimage and claim privkey were GC'd and the HTLC at the lockup address became unspendable from the user side. The Lightning HODL invoice times out after ~24h and Boltz refunds the LN side automatically — funds are not permanently lost but the user can't claim the on-chain side. **If you find a stuck swap from before this fix:** the secrets are unrecoverable; wait for the LN HODL refund (or open a Boltz Discord ticket with the swap ID asking them to cancel the HODL invoice early). See issue #481 for the historical context.

| Bottom sheet doesn't slide up when keyboard opens on Android
| All of these are required: (1) Use `BottomSheetTextInput` from `@gorhom/bottom-sheet` instead of React Native's `TextInput`. (2) Use `BottomSheetScrollView` (not `BottomSheetView`) as the content wrapper. (3) Set `keyboardBehavior="interactive"` (or `"extend"` if the sheet has multiple snap points and you want the IME to push it to the highest snap — see `GifPickerSheet`), plus `keyboardBlurBehavior="restore"` and `android_keyboardInputMode="adjustResize"` on the `BottomSheetModal`. (4) Ensure `android:windowSoftInputMode="adjustResize"` in AndroidManifest (via `plugins/withAdjustResize.js`). (5) Add a `Keyboard.addListener` to track keyboard height and set dynamic `paddingBottom` on the `contentContainerStyle` (e.g. `paddingBottom: keyboardHeight > 0 ? keyboardHeight + 80 : 40`). (6) Do NOT wrap content in `TouchableWithoutFeedback` with `Keyboard.dismiss` — this interferes with the sheet's keyboard handling. See `NostrLoginSheet.tsx` as the reference implementation.

| AVD soft keyboard never appears (focus an input → no IME, or IME blinks then dismisses)
| The Android emulator suppresses the on-screen IME when it detects a "hardware keyboard" (the AVD reports the host keyboard as attached). On API 35+ a second gate kicks in: `stylus_handwriting_enabled` defaults to `1`, which routes some focus events into the stylus-handwriting service instead of bringing up Gboard. Both gates regress on AVD restart unless re-applied. +
*Fix (apply BOTH; `setprop`-style settings persist across `am force-stop` but not across emulator boot):* +
[source,bash]
----
adb shell settings put secure show_ime_with_hard_keyboard 1
adb shell settings put secure stylus_handwriting_enabled 0
adb shell ime reset
adb shell ime enable com.google.android.inputmethod.latin/com.android.inputmethod.latin.LatinIME
adb shell ime set com.google.android.inputmethod.latin/com.android.inputmethod.latin.LatinIME
----
*Verify:* `adb shell dumpsys input_method | grep mInputShown` should report `mInputShown=true` once an input is focused. The audit script `scripts/keyboard-screenshot-audit.sh` polls this gate before every screenshot. +
*Lower-friction alternative:* run daily-driver dev work on **API 34, no Google Play, "Medium Phone" device profile**. API 34 doesn't ship the stylus-handwriting service so the second gate is a no-op, and the smaller GMS surface frees ~300 MB RAM (avoiding the "System UI isn't responding" stalls under memory pressure). API 36 is fine for production-build smoke testing — just expect to re-run both `settings put` commands after each cold boot.

| Android emulator pathologically slow + unstable (ANRs, "isn't responding" dialogs, Maestro driver-startup and `inputText` timeouts) while the HOST has free RAM and idle CPU
| Don't chase host RAM/CPU — the bottleneck is **disk I/O saturation from the emulator's own RAM-snapshot writeback**. Signature: host load average is high (~8) yet `top` shows the CPU mostly idle and memory free, because the load is processes blocked in **I/O wait**, not computing. +
*Diagnose (on the host):* +
[source,bash]
----
vmstat 1 3                 # bo (blocks-out) huge, wa (io-wait) 50-67%, b (blocked procs) high
cat /proc/pressure/io      # full avgN ~67%  -> tasks fully stalled on I/O
cat /proc/pressure/memory  # ~0.00           -> RAM is NOT the problem
pidstat -d 1 3 | grep qemu # qemu writing ~750 MB/s, sustained, even with an idle guest
----
*Root cause:* launching with `-no-snapshot-load` still keeps quickboot's **file-backed guest RAM**. `~/.android/avd/<AVD>.avd/snapshots/default_boot/ram.img` is sized to the guest RAM (4 GB for `-memory 4096`) and memory-mapped, so the kernel continuously flushes dirty RAM pages back to it (~750 MB/s observed) *even when the guest is idle*. That pins the disk at ~67% I/O-wait, and qemu then blocks on its own writes -> the guest freezes -> every downstream symptom (ANRs, Maestro `port 7001` driver-startup timeout, `inputText` gRPC `DEADLINE_EXCEEDED`). Confirm it's the writer: `ls -la <AVD>.avd/snapshots/default_boot/ram.img` shows its mtime advancing every second. +
*Fix:* relaunch with **`-no-snapshot`** (NOT `-no-snapshot-load`) so guest RAM is anonymous memory with no writeback: +
[source,bash]
----
adb emu kill
"$ANDROID_HOME/emulator/emulator" -avd <AVD> -no-snapshot -no-boot-anim -no-audio -memory 4096 -cores 4 -gpu swiftshader_indirect &
----
Measured impact: qemu writes dropped **~755 MB/s -> ~10 MB/s**, I/O pressure **67% -> 26%**, and the ANR / Maestro flakiness cleared. Trade-off: `-no-snapshot` disables quickboot, so every launch is a full cold boot (slower start, stable session). **This is the deeper cause behind the GMS-churn entry below — rule it out first.**

| Emulator throws repeated "System UI isn't responding" / "Process system isn't responding" / per-Google-app "isn't responding" ANR dialogs on the `google_apis` AVD (contact avatars never load in Messages, relays stall, Maestro flows die with `Terminated`)
| The android-34 **google_apis** system image bundles Google apps (Digital Wellbeing, Quicksearch / the Google app, Maps) and a full GMS that churn in the background under the software GPU (`-gpu swiftshader_indirect`), throwing ANR dialogs that steal window focus, starve the Nostr relay websockets + avatar image downloads (so Messages-list profile pictures stay on the default pig placeholder), and abort Maestro runs mid-flow. An in-guest System UI restart (`adb shell am force-stop com.android.systemui`) does **not** hold — it re-ANRs within seconds. First rule out the RAM-snapshot writeback issue above by doing a true cold boot (`adb emu kill`, then relaunch with `-no-snapshot`, not `-no-snapshot-load`); that often clears the ANRs, though the GMS churn can still return under load. +
*The biggest offender is core GMS, not the bundled apps.* The **"Process system isn't responding"** dialog specifically is `system_server` being pegged (~175% CPU) by `com.google.android.gms.persistent`, which loops ANR-ing and crashing on `.chimera.PersistentIntentOperationService` (`adb logcat \| grep "ANR in"` shows it). Disabling Wellbeing/Quicksearch/Maps quiets the focus-stealing app dialogs but NOT this one — you have to disable GMS itself to stop it. Confirmed harmless here: after disabling GMS, CPU dropped to ~370/400 idle, the ANRs stopped, and the app (which uses no GMS) ran fine. +
*The app needs none of Google Play Services*, so the image flavour is avoidable entirely: maps use MapLibre (`@maplibre/maplibre-react-native` — self-rendered OSM vector tiles, not Google Maps), notifications are local-first (`expo-notifications`; FCM is used only by the opt-in Marmot push — `googleServicesFile` in `app.config.ts`, with FCM auto-init off until the user turns push on), and `expo-location` runs on the platform provider — set the emulator's GPS with `adb emu geo fix 0.0589 52.2964` or Extended Controls → Location (the dev-pin env-var approach was removed in PR #597). Disabling GMS is fine for everything except that opt-in push: with GMS off the AVD can't get an FCM token, so Marmot push can't be tested on it (re-enable GMS, or use a phone). +
*Quick fix (reversible, keeps app data — re-apply after each cold boot, like the keyboard gates above):* +
[source,bash]
----
adb shell pm disable-user --user 0 com.google.android.gms                    # the big one — stops "Process system isn't responding"
adb shell pm disable-user --user 0 com.google.android.apps.wellbeing
adb shell pm disable-user --user 0 com.google.android.googlequicksearchbox
adb shell pm disable-user --user 0 com.google.android.apps.maps
# undo with: adb shell pm enable <package>
----
*Cleanest long-term:* build the test AVD from a Google-free image — `sdkmanager "system-images;android-34;aosp_atd;x86_64"` (AOSP Automated Test Device: no Google apps, lighter and faster for Maestro + CI). Caveat: a new AVD has a fresh Android Keystore, so a SecureStore/SQLCipher data backup from the old AVD won't decrypt onto it — re-seed the test account via `.maestro/common/login-as-piggy.yaml` / `.maestro/common/seed-test-wallet.yaml`.

| Cold-start "tap Send button feels frozen for ~12 s" but the Maestro perf script reports ~1 s
| Maestro taps immediately after `HomeScreen first render` (fast ~1 s on Pixel). The real freeze starts 3-7 s LATER when zap-counterparty / profile resolvers run N sequential relay round-trips on the JS thread. A user-driven tap lands INSIDE that window and waits up to 12 s for the queue to drain. +
*Diagnostic tools (all gated by `EXPO_PUBLIC_KEEP_PERF_LOGS=1` at build time):* +
1. **JS-thread heartbeat** — `perfHeartbeatStart()` in `src/utils/perfLog.ts` self-schedules every 100 ms and logs the gap between scheduled-fire and actual-fire. Gap > 200 ms is a stutter; > 1 s is a freeze. Captures freezes without needing a tap. +
2. **Multi-window tap-latency script** — `scripts/perf-cold-tap-windows.sh` cold-starts once per offset and taps Send at +0/+500/+1.5k/+3k/+5k/+7k/+10k/+13k ms after Home renders. Plots tap-to-onPress latency as a curve so the freeze window is visible. +
3. **`perfLog(tag)`** sprinkled at every cold-start boundary (`WalletProvider first render`, `HomeScreen first render`, `WalletCarousel first render (N wallets)`, `TransactionList first render (N txs)`, `resolveZapSenders[id]: start/done`, etc.). A single `adb logcat \| grep "\[Perf\]"` gives the full attribution. +
*Common cause + fix:* sequential `await fetchProfile(pk, relays)` in a `for...of` loop over N transactions. Each iteration is one relay round-trip (~500-2000 ms). Replace with two-phase batch: collect all pubkeys → one `zapSenderProfileStorage.getMany([...pks])` from disk → one `nostrService.fetchProfiles(missing, relays)` for the remainder. The incoming branch of `resolveZapSendersForWallet` already used this pattern; the outgoing branch did not until PR #510. +
*Build a perf-instrumented APK to investigate on a real device:* `EXPO_PUBLIC_KEEP_PERF_LOGS=1 eas build --local --profile production --platform android --non-interactive --output /tmp/lp-perf.apk`. `babel.config.js` skips `transform-remove-console` and `perfLog.ts` honours the same env flag, so `[Perf]` lines survive in the release APK. **Never ship a real release build with the flag set.**

| `npx expo start` fails but `npm start` works
| The `start` script includes `--dev-client` which is required for custom native modules. Always use `npm start`.

| EAS local production build crashes mid-Gradle with `UncheckedIOException: Could not add entry ... to cache fileHashes.bin`
| `/tmp` is on tmpfs (default Ubuntu/Debian) capped at ~12 GB. The EAS local builder generates ~9 GB of native-lib intermediates during the multi-arch APK assembly (arm64 + armv7 + x86 + x86_64). When tmpfs hits 100 %, Gradle's incremental-cache writer throws on the next `fileHashes.bin` flush and the entire build aborts. +
*Symptom:* `tail -3 /tmp/eas-build.log` shows the IOException; `df -h /tmp` shows tmpfs at 100 %; the build PID is gone with no APK at `android/app/build/outputs/apk/release/app-release.apk`. +
*Fix:* point Gradle at disk-backed storage via `TMPDIR`: +
[source,bash]
----
mkdir -p "$HOME/eas-tmp"
TMPDIR="$HOME/eas-tmp" EXPO_NO_DOCTOR=1 \
  eas build --local --platform android --profile production --non-interactive
----
*Cleanup:* `rm -rf "$HOME/eas-tmp"` between builds when you want to reclaim disk; the next run's `mkdir -p` and Gradle will re-create everything, including hidden entries such as `.gradle` or `.expo`. The EAS-CLI checkout under `$TMPDIR/eas-build-local-nodejs/` is also safe to delete between runs. Full recipe + rationale in `docs/DEPLOYMENT.adoc` → "Local production builds".

| `eas build --local --profile production` produces an APK with a versionCode that won't install on the target device, and `eas build:version:set` doesn't fix it
| `eas.json` has `cli.appVersionSource: "remote"` + production profile `autoIncrement: true`. **This row is specifically about `eas build --local`** — `expo run:android --variant release` is a different path that reads `versionCode` straight from `app.config.ts` (see `app.config.ts:135-148` and `docs/DEPLOYMENT.adoc` → "Local production builds" — that mechanism is not what's broken here). +
The `--local` path is supposed to share the EAS server counter that `eas build:version:set/get` operates on. In practice (verified 2026-05-05 against eas-cli@18.11.0) it has its own per-machine counter that walks +1 per build regardless of any `:set`: `:get` reported the production counter at 50, but four successive local builds produced versionCodes 28, 29, 30, 31. Two further sharp edges: +
*1. The local-build counter walks +1 per build with no way to push it forward via `:set`.* The only way to clear an underwater `eas build --local` versionCode is to keep building until it climbs past the device floor (~20 min per build) or use a cloud build instead. `eas build --profile production --platform android` (no `--local`) does honour the same counter `:get` reports. +
*2. The device floor is sticky.* Once you install versionCode N on a device, you cannot downgrade to versionCode < N without uninstalling (which loses all app data: wallets, Nostr login, message history). `adb install -r -d` (allow-downgrade) is a no-op for non-debuggable production APKs on Android 14+. So if a local production build ever ends up far below the device floor, you have to either (a) burn enough local builds to climb past the floor, (b) switch to a cloud build, or (c) wipe the app. *Don't* try to bypass via `appVersionSource: "local"` for `eas build --local` — that breaks the EAS-managed keystore path and the next build signs with a local debug keystore, producing `INSTALL_FAILED_UPDATE_INCOMPATIBLE` on top of everything else. +
*Mitigation:* `scripts/eas-deploy-pixel.sh` reads the device's current versionCode, kicks off `eas build --local`, watches the build log for the `[CONFIGURE_ANDROID_VERSION] Version code: NN` line (which appears ~60 sec into a 20-min build), and aborts the whole build tree (eas-cli + npm-exec + Gradle daemon + Kotlin daemon) if `NN <= device`. Saves ~19 min per doomed build. Use it instead of running raw `eas build --local` for ad-hoc Pixel deploys. +
*Symptom + diagnosis sequence:* +
[source,bash]
----
adb -s <serial> install -r build-*.apk
# → Failure [INSTALL_FAILED_VERSION_DOWNGRADE: Update version code 28 is older than current 34]
adb -s <serial> shell dumpsys package com.lightningpiggy.app | grep -E versionCode      # device floor
APP_VARIANT=production eas build:version:get --platform android --profile production    # `:get` counter
grep -oP 'Version code: \K\d+' /tmp/eas-prod-build.log | head -1                         # what the local build actually used
----
The third number is the one to compare against the device floor — *not* the second, even though Expo docs imply they're the same. The APK in hand cannot be re-stamped — `versionCode` is signed into the manifest, repackaging breaks the EAS keystore signature.

| In-app feature reads `process.env.EXPO_PUBLIC_*` as `undefined` on a release/preview build even though the emulator (Metro) has it, e.g. GIF picker shows *"The GIPHY API key hasn't been set for this build"* on the installed APK but works fine in dev

----

| Android emulator "System UI isn't responding" / app stuck on splash
| The AVD is thrashing on RAM. Symptoms in `adb logcat`: `kswapd0` burning 40%+ kernel CPU, `MemFree` under 100 MB, `system_server` over 100% CPU, Binder IPC failing with `No space left on device` (which is a RAM-pressure symptom, not disk). | On Android, `getCurrentPositionAsync` goes through `FusedLocationProviderClient`, which returns the *cached* fix and throws `ERR_CURRENT_LOCATION_IS_UNAVAILABLE` if the cache is empty — it does not block waiting for a GNSS fix. On emulators the cache is empty until something is actively listening. +
Symptoms reproduce even after: granting `ACCESS_FINE_LOCATION` (`pm grant com.lightningpiggy.app.dev android.permission.ACCESS_FINE_LOCATION`), enabling location services (`settings get secure location_mode` returns `3`), pushing a one-shot fix (`adb emu geo fix <lon> <lat>`), and loading + playing a route in Android Studio's *Extended Controls → Location* panel. Setting `hw.keyboard=no` removes that flag from the AVD's reported hardware spec, BUT — and this is the gotcha — QEMU still attaches a generic `AT Translated Set 2 keyboard` device to the VM regardless. So Android's `InputManagerService` ALWAYS sees a "physical keyboard". The settings override (`Settings.Secure.show_ime_with_hard_keyboard=1`) is supposed to force Gboard despite that, but in practice this is intermittent on the Pixel-8 / Android 16 system image — Gboard reads the setting lazily and the IMM caches its IME-binding state across IME process restarts, so toggling the setting while the system is running often does nothing. +
*Initial fix (works briefly):* edit the AVD config to drop the explicit hw-keyboard flag, then **cold-boot** the emulator and ensure the override is set BEFORE the first input focus:
[source,bash]
----
# `sed -i` is portable across GNU (Linux) and BSD (macOS) sed only if
# you branch on `uname` — BSD sed requires an explicit (possibly empty)
# backup-suffix argument after `-i`, GNU sed forbids it.
if [ "$(uname)" = "Darwin" ]; then
  sed -i '' 's/^hw\.keyboard=yes$/hw.keyboard=no/' ~/.android/avd/<AVD_NAME>.avd/config.ini
else
  sed -i 's/^hw\.keyboard=yes$/hw.keyboard=no/' ~/.android/avd/<AVD_NAME>.avd/config.ini
fi
adb -s emulator-5554 emu kill
$ANDROID_HOME/emulator/emulator -avd <AVD_NAME> -no-snapshot-load &
adb wait-for-device
adb shell settings put secure show_ime_with_hard_keyboard 1
----
After cold boot, tapping a text input shows the full docked Gboard at the bottom and `react-native-keyboard-controller` reports the correct IME height. +
*Important: this is NOT a permanent fix.* `adb shell dumpsys input` after cold boot still shows `Device 2: AT Translated Set 2 keyboard` regardless of the AVD setting — QEMU always attaches a virtual PS/2 keyboard. Over the life of an emulator session the floating IME chooser will return after some combination of: re-launching the app, switching IMEs, running Maestro tests that programmatically inject input, or simply re-focusing an input after backgrounding. When this happens, you have to cold-boot again — `adb reboot`, force-stopping LatinIME, re-toggling `show_ime_with_hard_keyboard`, and `am force-stop` of the system UI all fail to recover the docked keyboard once the IMM has cached the suppressed state. +
*Side effect:* with `hw.keyboard=no`, the laptop keyboard no longer types into the emulator. For interactive development this is annoying — your two paths are: (a) tap-input via mouse on the emulator window and use Gboard, or (b) inject text from the host CLI with `adb -s emulator-5554 shell input text "your%smessage"` (`%s` = space). For Maestro tests, prefer the `inputText` action which doesn't depend on a visible IME. +
*Workaround when neither approach works:* if you need both laptop typing AND a working IME, run on a real device. For Maestro tests run inside CI, use `inputText` exclusively and never assert on the keyboard's visible bounds — only on the visibility / position of the composer above where the IME *would* be. +
*Diagnostic:* after cold boot `adb shell dumpsys input_method | grep mInputShown` should be `true` while focused, and `adb shell dumpsys input | grep -E "AT Translated|qwerty"` will always show the AT keyboard regardless. The reliable test is whether tapping an input renders the docked Gboard pixels — there is no clean adb signal for "Gboard is rendered vs the floating chooser is rendered".

| Emulator boots to a grey screen / `boot_completed` never flips to 1 / `adb devices` flaps `device` ↔ `offline` indefinitely

[source,bash]

#    The base *.img files are NOT touched — they regenerate the
#    overlays on next boot.

rm ~/.android/avd/<AVD_NAME>.avd/cache.img.qcow2

rm -f ~/.android/avd/<AVD_NAME>.avd/quickbootChoice.ini
rm -rf ~/.android/avd/<AVD_NAME>.avd/snapshots
rm -rf ~/.android/avd/<AVD_NAME>.avd/tmpAdbCmds

*What you lose:* every app installed inside the AVD, signed-in accounts, AVD-side preferences. The AVD config (`config.ini`, `hardware-qemu.ini`, `AVD.conf`) and base system image are preserved, so the AVD itself stays valid. +
*What you gain:* the `userdata-qemu.img.qcow2` file goes from a multi-GB accumulated overlay (often 7 GB+ after months of use) back to a thin overlay regenerated from the system image's userdata. +
*Don't waste time on these false leads first:* (a) `adb reboot` won't help — the corrupt overlay is what re-mounts on every boot. (b) Switching GPU mode won't help — the GPU init phase succeeds; the failure is in Android userspace boot. (c) Increasing AVD RAM won't help — `system_server` isn't OOM, it's hanging on the corrupt overlay. (d) Re-running `expo prebuild` won't help — this is an emulator state issue, not an app build issue. +
*Verify clean boot:* `adb -s emulator-5554 shell getprop sys.boot_completed` returns `1` (with a stable trailing newline) and `adb devices` shows `device` (not `offline`) consistently for >30 s. UI rendering should be smooth — if you see frame stutters and a `Process system isn't responding` dialog, you're probably on `swiftshader_indirect` (software GL) and need to re-launch with `-gpu host` now that the overlay is clean.

=== Marmot invitation appears on another device

A Marmot invitation targets the private material belonging to one published
invitation key, not every phone signed into the same Nostr account. Lightning
Piggy invites every fresh, compatible kind-30443 key package of a contact (one
per `d` slot, newest first, at most 10; White Noise slots up to 35 days old,
others up to 14; each must advertise encrypted media v2, `0x800b`). For a 1:1
with a White Noise user it invites only their newest White Noise key, because
White Noise counts MLS leaves to decide what is a DM. A signed-out or unused
installation can still attract invitations until its key is removed or expires.

White Noise silently drops a Welcome whose group contains any member leaf
without encrypted media v2 (`0x800b`) — no error, no invite row. If a group
started from Lightning Piggy never appears in White Noise, check whether one of
the invited key packages (often an old test install) lacks `0x800b` in its
`app_components` tag.

For White Noise Android 2026.10.3 (MDK 0.12.0, revision `7692e266`), peer key
packages are discovered through kind-10002 write relays. The Welcome is a
kind-444 rumor inside a kind-1059 gift wrap addressed to the recipient, delivered
to kind-10050 inbox relays. Kind 10051 is not required. Check which installation
published the selected key before changing the transport or assuming the
Welcome was lost.

On 2026-10-10, Big Piggy's Lightning Piggy-initiated invitation from the hulk
emulator appeared in Little Piggy's White Noise on the main emulator, including
the decrypted first message and Accept button. Hulk's White Noise onboarding
had not published a new key. Checking hulk alone incorrectly suggested that
Lightning Piggy-initiated Welcomes were incompatible.

Lightning Piggy refreshes this installation's key weekly in the same `d` slot.
The random slot id is per account per installation and survives account
sign-out and re-sign-in (two accounts on one install never share one).
Once a replacement is confirmed published, a USED superseded secret is
deleted at once (spec). An UNUSED one is kept until its own `not_after`
(at most 84 days), matching MDK / White Noise, so a Welcome made with the
previous key package — say for someone who was offline over the weekly
refresh — still joins (Ben's retention decision, #1230, extended
2026-10-14). That stays inside the bound `foundation/key-packages.md` sets
(retention never extends past the key package's lifetime). Older slots are retired
with NIP-09 only when local publication records establish that this install
owns them; a matching public client tag does not establish ownership.

If one installation logs several joins for the same group ("ghost leaves"),
compare the stored Welcome commit fingerprints. Inviters send a separate
rumor for each slot, but the MLS Welcome can be identical and contain the
secret for this install in every copy. Rumor-id dedupe alone therefore cannot
prevent rejoining. Welcome adoption is serialized, and a copy whose commit
fingerprint (sha256 of the encrypted GroupInfo) matches a group this session
already joined is ignored without any MLS work — `readInviteGroupInfo` is a
full `joinGroup` in marmot-ts, so it is never run ahead of a join. A copy from
a later commit still fails inside the library ("already exists") and is then
recorded as joined. The stack's SelfRemove operates on its tracked membership; it
cannot safely remove an extra leaf without first overwriting that membership.
Account removal waits for pending key publication, requests deletion, and
publishes an empty replacement for each local slot before wiping local state.
Relay deletion is best-effort; a warning means old advertisements may remain.

=== White Noise stops receiving a Marmot group after a rename or admin change

Symptom: messages before a Lightning Piggy rename or admin change reach White
Noise, but nothing after it does, and White Noise keeps the old name (#1309).

Cause: OpenMLS (MDK, so White Noise) applies an `app_data_update` by removing
the `app_data_dictionary` GroupContext extension and re-appending it, while
ts-mls (marmot-ts) replaces it in place. marmot-ts creates groups with the
dictionary first (MDK puts it last), so after the commit the two sides derive
different GroupContexts. MDK rejects it with `Confirmation tag mismatch` and
stays on the old epoch. White Noise release builds don't log this. MDK's own
`wn`/`wnd` CLI built from the White Noise pin (`7692e266`) shows it in the
`openmls::group::mls_group::staged_commit` trace.

The reverse direction breaks the same way. When a White Noise admin renames
an unaligned Lightning Piggy group, OpenMLS moves the dictionary to the end,
and Lightning Piggy rejects White Noise's commit and stops receiving the group.

Fix: every commit Lightning Piggy sends goes through `MarmotSession.sendIntent`,
including the invite that founds a group. While a group isn't aligned, that
commit also carries a GroupContextExtensions proposal that re-sends the same
extensions with the dictionary last (`alignCommitForMdk` /
`proposeAppDataDictionaryLast` in `marmotProposals.ts`). The decision is made
on the group's state at the moment of submitting. So:

* New groups are aligned by their first invite, before a White Noise admin can
  commit, which covers both directions.
* Existing groups are aligned by Lightning Piggy's next rename, admin change,
  member removal or invite. Until then a White Noise admin's rename still splits
  the Lightning Piggy members off.
* A commit that would bundle a staged GroupContextExtensions or `app_data_update`
  proposal skips the realign, which a later commit does instead.

Groups White Noise created are already aligned. A group whose metadata commit
was already rejected by either side stays split: start a new group.

=== iOS simulator keeps loading the main Metro instead of a worktree's

The iOS dev client reads its bundle host from React Native's `RCT_jsLocation`

=== Messages refreshes repeatedly while a Marmot thread shows a spinner

The 2026-10-12 Pixel log showed four 11.6–16.0 s inbox refreshes, each
replaying 1,354 kind-4 events and 225 wraps. The store skipped 1,219 kind-4
rows and 200 wraps, but each pass still attempted 25 un-stored wraps.
Automatic JS/native reconnect callbacks used `force: true`, bypassing both
the freshness throttle and the negative wrap cache and dropping the kind-4
cursor. Groups focus and map location catch-up also forced full refreshes.
Automatic reconciliation now keeps the cursor and shares a 60 s completion
TTL; explicit pull-to-refresh/filter changes and the one cold backfill retain
their bypasses. Native reconnect bursts share one cancellable 3 s settle timer. A newly
widened non-follow view backfills kind-4 history without `since`, including
messages older than the 120 s overlap. Suppressed reconciliation schedules one
account-scoped retry at TTL expiry, cancelled on account switch/unmount or a
new pass. The retry retains the kind-4 cursor and negative wrap cache.
Refresh callers wait for setup before inspecting the effective abort signal;
forced/backfill requests await weaker work and then run their own pass.
Productive aborts still persist/paint partial batches but preserve both cursors.
Reconnects joining an active refresh retain one reconciliation after that flight;
its completion TTL still applies, including when reconnect happens after fetch.
Null kind-4 decrypts count as processed and enter a bounded account/session
retry store (user refreshes retry them). After three ciphertext failures,
twenty transient failures or seven days, they stop pinning the cursor.
Capacity eviction must first move the id to the separately persisted,
5,000-id exhausted cache; otherwise overlap replays reset its counters and age.
If all 500 retry entries are pending, retire the oldest floor first so the
cursor progresses. Exhausted-cache overflow discards the oldest history.
The regression replays 501 failures spanning 50 seconds over 11,000 one-minute
passes, including restarts, and checks that the cursor never regresses and
retries stop for all three ceilings. They cannot keep refreshes cold or
block full wrap backfill. Unattempted messages and failed database writes still
preserve the history cursor.

Kind-4 retry exhaustion is persisted with a monotonic replay cursor. Capacity
is derived from two kind-4 filters, their fetch limit and the unique relay
count, rather than the former 500/5,000-entry caps. A negative may be forgotten
only when its timestamp is strictly older than cursor minus the 120-second
replay overlap (the relay's `since` is inclusive). If exhausted entries still
exceed capacity inside that window, the refresh proposes a cursor beyond the
oldest exhausted timestamps and stages pruning in an isolated retry store.
Only after every targeted kind-4 row is accounted for and all row writes
succeed does the refresh commit cursor, retries and exhausted IDs in one
versioned AsyncStorage value (`dm-kind4-retries:<owner>`). Abort, ingestion
exceptions and failed DB or metadata writes discard the proposal, leaving
the committed cursor and metadata unchanged. Relay queries and retry scheduling
read committed state; legacy retry, exhausted and inbox cursor keys are
migrated on the first successful commit. Regression tests cover overflow
with abort after 15 of 16 targets, partial DB write failure, metadata write
failure, ingestion exceptions and successful commits, in addition to
5,501 failures within 50 seconds with a save/restart every pass, as well as
pressure beyond the new fetch-derived capacity.

Do not interpret `nostrCrypto schnorrVerify n=1888` as 1,888 checks per
refresh: `n` is cumulative for the process, while percentiles cover only the
last 200 samples. It increased by just one between 21:07:09 and 21:08:09.
The verifier is synchronous, so other JS callbacks cannot interleave inside
its timing bracket; CPU scheduling/GC can still inflate wall time. Memoised
nostr-tools checks no longer add samples. The bounded verified-id cache is
now LRU so replayed inbox ids survive unrelated feed churn; cache hits still
check the canonical content hash. Plaintext DB rows are not signature proofs
and must not seed that cache.

Marmot thread navigation does not await the global refresh or MLS readiness:
`useConversationLoader` reads encrypted cached rows, paints them, and receives
coalesced store notifications as session hydration finishes. It now performs
one store read per load instead of two. A JS-thread stall can still delay the
first store continuation/paint. With no device capture, the originating
trigger and the cost of those 25 retrying wraps cannot be conclusively
attributed from these markers alone.

=== Marmot sync stops after backgrounding

Marmot's kind-445 group subscriptions use the shared JS SimplePool, whose
`enableReconnect` is false. A socket drop used to leave the MLS connection
record alive with a dead transport; the MarmotBridge resume handler only
checked key-package freshness. `marmotSubscription.ts` now retries relay
failures with capped backoff and replaces the cached SimplePool transport
three seconds after resume: closing a subscription alone leaves a silently
dead WebSocket cached. Both paths reconnect the session through
`GroupsManager.connectAll`, which collects every bounded backfill page and
ingests the complete ordered MLS batch before opening live delivery. This
recovers offline gaps beyond 500 events, including older epoch commits.
Backoff survives replacement of the group connections. Backgrounding cancels
pending timers; account teardown invalidates callbacks and closes routes.
Group filters retain their watermark window and relay fan-out dedupe.
Merged group REQs (#1399, `marmotGroupSubscriptions.ts`) are not per-relay
routes, so `createMarmotNetwork` owns their resume: one `watchMarmotResume`
watcher per network replaces every group relay's transport after the same
three-second stagger and then reconnects the session once; a lost group socket
re-arms the merged REQ with exponential backoff (one second doubling to a
minute; it resets only after an arm stayed open for 30 seconds, so a relay
that accepts the REQ and CLOSEs it at once keeps backing off) and runs per-group paged recovery, notifying
the Welcome retry listener once its groups have caught up.

Transport replacement isolates each SDK connection attempt's relay registry:
a retired connecting publish timing out must not delete the fresh relay at
the same URL. Explicit pool `close` and `destroy` invalidate affected active
and queued subscriptions before closing sockets. Teardown generations also
prevent pending connections or resets from reviving those routes; unrelated
subscriptions and deliberate requests made after teardown remain usable.
Closing one relay in a multi-relay subscription preserves its healthy routes
and excludes the closed route from later resume restoration. Direct
`ensureRelay()` / `relay.subscribe()` reads (reactions and contact lists) drain
on the retired socket until their original subscription closes, so replacement
does not turn an in-flight authoritative read into a failure. Their existing
read deadlines bound the drain; explicit pool close/destroy still releases
retired sockets.

Welcomes (kind 444 inside kind 1059) arrive through the ordinary DM inbox,
not a separate key-package inbox. The JS DM controller already rearms on
resume. The native adapter now replaces its pool immediately on resume and
seeds the new subscription with current known wrap ids, without a `since`
filter. Native reconnect/resume reconciliation still waits three seconds
and respects the shared automatic-refresh interval. If native resume fails,
the live-DM bridge restores the JS wrap subscription.

A transient join failure leaves the decrypted Welcome in `pendingWelcomes`,
while the DM inbox has already marked its wrap processed. The session now
retries those rows at startup, three seconds after app resume, and after a
Marmot relay route recovers (replay or EOSE). Retry scans are single-flight,
with at most three passes per trigger and exponential backoff from five
seconds capped at five minutes. Repeated reconnect signals cannot bypass
backoff. Backgrounding cancels queued work and session teardown removes the
listeners. Each pass uses `acceptWelcome`, preserving the in-flight guard
and durable outcome dedupe; successful or permanent outcomes remove the row.

Marmot first shipped in PR #1163 after v1.4.1. PR #1417 changed automatic
inbox reconciliation policy, not the group subscription lifecycle. A lack
of `[Marmot]` log output alone cannot distinguish a dead group transport
from an undelivered Welcome, a follow-gated sender, or a transient join error.

=== Marmot install cannot be invited again after joining

appears in the same `d` slot, multi-device inviters correctly skip it. Earlier
builds attempted maintenance once after joining but did not retry publication

== Missing Welcomes or DMs with "too many concurrent REQs" (#1399)

A relay may reject the gift-wrap inbox when per-group and temporary queries
exhaust its concurrent-subscription limit. This is especially visible when
other configured relays are unreachable or IP-banned. Event `limit` bounds
history; it does not limit the number of open subscriptions.

Use a build containing the shared pool's REQ scheduler and merged Marmot
subscriptions. In dev/performance-log builds, verify `[Relay] budget`,
`[Marmot] inbox armed on`, and `[Marmot] group subs merged:` markers.
The inbox should arm at login, even before opening Messages. Limit notices
cause retries with backoff and a reduced budget. See PERFORMANCE.adoc,
"Relay subscription capacity", for the fake-pool counts and grep command.
A replay ending at 512 log lines alone does not establish a paging failure:
the group backfill uses bounded 500-event pages and has a >512-event test.

If groups never receive live events while the inbox remains active, inspect
`group subs merged` and the budget's queued owners. Per-group-filter chunks
consume separate live slots; equally prioritised tail chunks can stay queued
indefinitely. Merge group ids into one live-only `#h` filter with a 60-second overlap and
limit 100. Split only at 256 ids per filter or the 64 KiB message bound,
keeping split filters in one physical REQ when possible; larger sets rotate
every ten seconds on one group slot. Historical replay must never use a
combined limit: 1,000 newer busy-group events can hide a quiet group's MLS
commit, and relays can clamp limits further. Subscribe, scheduler resume and
socket reconnect instead run independent paged per-group recovery as budgeted
one-shots from each group's cursor. Live delivery waits behind its group's
recovery. A pass that reaches the 40-page cap with older events unread (a relay
clamping pages to 100) delivers nothing and continues below its oldest event on
the next pass, so the cursor never skips the oldest commits. Memory is bounded
at 20,000 events per group by retaining the oldest: the dropped newer events
are re-read from the advanced cursor after that range is delivered. A
duplicate-only page as full as any the relay served (more events in one second
than a page holds) is stepped past with a warning instead of ending the range.
A recovery read succeeds once any relay reaches EOSE and fails only when none
does, so a dead or AUTH-gated relay in a group's list (the health layer always
keeps two) cannot hold the group's live delivery. Live events buffered behind
recovery are bounded to 1,000 distinct ids; on overflow the buffer is dropped
and the next pass re-reads them from the unchanged cursor. Regressions cover
the quiet commit behind 1,001 busy events, clamped pages past the page cap, a
25,000-event history, saturated seconds, one dead relay of two, buffer
overflow, all 41 groups at limit 4, splitting above 256 ids, re-arm backoff,
reconnect and cleanup.



=== Budget stays at 10/10 and Welcomes never join

Live subscriptions occupying all slots can starve group-state reads and Welcome
ownership verification in the failure path. A queued verification delays the
Welcome failure log; a successful MLS join uses local key material. The absence
of both join and failure logs alone does not identify the stopped await.
Use the reservation-aware scheduler: at limit 10, at most 7 live REQs may open,
leaving 3 slots for one-shots. One-shots close per relay at EOSE and have an
independent 20-second deadline covering queueing and connection time. Raw
snapshot reads and profile batches follow that lifecycle too. Socket close
settles queued owners and releases every slot.

Grep `[Relay] budget stall` for the oldest queued label and holder breakdown,
and `[Relay] budget timeout` for forced recovery. Live group logs should show
`N groups -> 1 REQs` per relay, including more than 100 groups. Welcome lookups
combine their filters into one REQ and joins reuse the decrypted rumor and local
keys. The no-network regression registers 10 live owners, admits only 7, then
runs three one-shots without exceeding the relay cap.

=== An account switch must not change a pending DM's relay policy

NIP-17 nsec sends yield between wraps; external-signer sends await encryption
and signing. Lock relay destinations to the sender identity before those
waits. Reading only the currently active account at publish time can let an
old TEST account's pending wraps escape to public relays after an account
switch. `nostrNip17Send` retains the sender's locked relay snapshot for the
whole send, including delivery tracking and stale-socket retries.

=== Pending profile publishes or market connects cross an account switch

A profile publish may resume after SecureStore or external signing completes.

health wrapper and the SDK's internal `ensureRelay` call; active-account

=== Auth-only relay reads finish empty or report an unhandled AUTH send rejection

In nostr-tools 2.23.3, CLOSED leaves a subscription's EOSE timer running.
A slow NIP-42 signer can let that timer finish a one-shot read empty or close
its authenticated replacement. `relayReqTransport` invalidates callbacks from
each retired physical request before awaiting AUTH.

The SDK also ignores the promise returned by AUTH `send()`. A disconnect
while signing retains the challenge, so checking only the challenge can send
on a closed connection and expose the signed event in an unhandled rejection.
`relayAuth` captures the originating socket/connection, cancels signing on its
close event, and guards the AUTH send through the SDK's existing OK waiter.
Send failures clear that waiter and reject with a sanitized error; late signer
completion and reconnects cannot authenticate the retired socket.


=== strfry auth-only reads never authenticate

strfry can close a restricted REQ with
`ERROR: auth-required: this event may only be read by its recipients`.
Checking only the start of the reason misses its `ERROR:` wrapper.
`relayReqTransport` recognises `auth-required:` at colon-separated token
boundaries, case-insensitively; `relayQuery` does the same for authentication
and `restricted:` refusals so failed reads cannot appear to succeed empty.

nostr-tools retains AUTH challenges received on connect, before any CLOSED.
The transport uses that challenge when the first auth-required CLOSED arrives,
signs once, and re-arms once after successful AUTH. A second auth-required or
restricted CLOSED ends the read without another authentication attempt.
